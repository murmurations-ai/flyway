# Defining Source

flyway uses the word "Source" in a specific, load-bearing sense — the role
that holds authority, vision, and accountability for a creative endeavor.
This is not casual jargon. It is drawn from a body of work originated by
**Peter Koenig** and popularized by **Tom Nixon**, and the precision of
the term is what lets flyway speak coherently about sovereignty,
recognition, and exit between independent murmurations.

## The concept

A Source is the person who first takes the initiative and the risk on an
idea — the founder of an organization, the convener of a project, the
originator of a venture. The Source holds:

- The **vision** of what the initiative is trying to accomplish
- The **authority** to make calls about scope, direction, and membership
- The **accountability** for the initiative's outcomes
- The **creative responsibility** that no one else can substitute for

The role is real whether or not it is named. In Koenig and Nixon's
analysis, much organizational dysfunction comes from confusion about who
the Source is, or from Sources who try to disown the role rather than
hold it consciously.

A few specific patterns from this body of work that matter for flyway:

- **Specific Source.** Every initiative has exactly one Source. Sources
  cannot be split or shared between two people; trying to do so produces
  the dysfunction it appears to avoid.
- **Sub-Sources.** Within a larger initiative, smaller initiatives have
  their own (sub-)Sources who hold authority over their own scope while
  remaining under the larger initiative's Source.
- **Inheritance.** Source can be passed on, but only by deliberate
  succession — not by absence, abandonment, or assumption.

## Origin and key thinkers

**Peter Koenig** is a Swiss-German consultant who developed Source
Principles through decades of organizational and personal-development
work, particularly around money, value, and the inner game of leadership.
His foundational book is *30 Lies About Money* (2003); the deeper
elaboration of Source theory has come through his workshops, mentoring,
and the lineage of practitioners trained directly by him.

**Tom Nixon** popularized and extended Koenig's work for an
organizational-design audience. His book *Work with Source: Realise Big
Ideas, Organise for Emergence and Lead from Your True Self* (2021) is the
most accessible introduction and the canonical English-language reference.
Nixon is a UK-based consultant and a co-founder of Maptio, a tool for
visualizing accountability in organizations.

## How flyway uses "Source"

In flyway, every murmuration has exactly one Source — a human who
controls the agent swarm, holds its accountability, and decides what it
does and whom it federates with. The Source is the entity flyway treats
as sovereign.

Wherever flyway invariants speak of "Source authority," "Source
sovereignty," or "your Source," they are using the term in the
Koenig/Nixon sense: the human who initiated the murmuration and bears
its outcomes.

This conceptual grounding is why flyway is shaped the way it is:

- **Identity** anchors at the Source's repository, not at any agent,
  runtime, or installation. A Source can switch from Claude Code to the
  harness without changing identity, because the identity belongs to the
  Source, not the tool.
- **Recognition** happens between Sources, not between agents.
- **Agreements** are signed by Sources, or by agents acting under
  explicitly delegated Source authority.
- **Exit** is always valid because exit is the Source's prerogative.
  Nothing in flyway can override that.

## Why this framing matters for an agent protocol

Most multi-agent protocols collapse the human role into "the user" or
"the operator." flyway treats it as a richer, named role with specific
responsibilities, drawing on a body of practice that has been refined for
decades in organizational design.

The choice is deliberate. In a world of increasingly autonomous agents,
naming who actually holds the authority and the accountability — and
grounding cross-system identity in *that* — is the only stable
foundation. Without a clear Source, a murmuration cannot give consent,
cannot exit cleanly, and cannot be held to its agreements.

A murmuration whose Source has not engaged with what it means to *be*
Source is unlikely to produce the considered consent and clean exit that
flyway depends on. The conceptual work is upstream of the protocol work.
